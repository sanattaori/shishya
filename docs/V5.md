# Shishya v5: a memory that keeps meaning (plan and pass rules)

*Written 1 Nov 2026, 23:50 IST, on branch `brain-v5` (the other session's v4/v5 work plus the hybrid). Written
after the smoke checks below and before any 40-person dev school, any dev life and any test run. The rules and
predictions don't change after results are seen. Code: `brain/v5.py`.*

## Why v5

Every version so far stores a fact well and recalls it when asked in the **same words**, and fails when asked in
**other words**:

| | Same words | Other words (never seen) | Two steps |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| v4, BDH Brain, life test (RESULTS.md section 8) | 98% | 35% | |
| v4, BDH Brain, school, 40 people (section 9) | 100% | 22% | 24% |
| Hybrid stage 1: SmolLM2 + v3's memory (docs/HYBRID.md) | 96% | 27% | |
| RAG on SmolLM2 (same test) | 100% | 100% | |

Two findings point at the cause:
- **The address is the wording.** v3/v4 address a fact by the words of its sentence. Another wording shares a
  third of the address. (The school's probe: v4 finds the right fact for 76% of unseen wordings, but shares only
  about 30% of its address, so the read is faint.)
- **Keeping the strongest 40 of 16,384 units amplifies small differences** (v5 probe, `brain/out/v5/probe.json`,
  dev names). Two "who" codes about 95% alike as dense vectors kept only 31% of their units in common.

## The design

v5 is a frozen LLM (SmolLM2-135M, as in the hybrid) plus a new memory. Its address has two parts:

- **who** picks the memory's units: the words of the sentence so far, each counting by its novelty to the power 16,
  so only new names count. That gives the strongest 40 of 16,384 units. The same names give the same units, in
  any wording.
- **what** is 32 numbers: the LLM's hidden state at one layer, over the sentence read alone, through a projection
  `W` (576 × 32) learned in childhood. It says what the sentence is about to name.

Each unit holds a 32 × 128 matrix (what → value), 268 MB in all, fixed. The value is the next token, as a fixed
random projection of its embedding. Two addresses match by (who overlap) × (what cosine). There is no top-k step on
the meaning, so a question in other words reads its fact smoothly, not faintly.

- **A gate:** a step is written and read only when its sentence so far has a new word (novelty¹⁶ ≥ 0.5). Ordinary
  text is never written, so the LLM's reading of it should be untouched.
- **Life:** as v3, with no gradients. The exact sequential delta rule, β = min(rate × surprise × reward, 1).
  Surprise is the LLM's loss on the next token (the sentence read alone, with the memory), divided by its average.
- **Childhood (once, LLM frozen):** learns only `W`, the write rate and the read gain (18,434 numbers). It trains
  on made-up worlds: 8 people × 5 facts, each fact taught once in one wording and asked in another (2 of 3) or
  the same (1 of 3).
  - The names are train names only.
  - **26 relation types (teacher, boat, school, song, …) that are not the tests' five** (pet, town, best friend,
    toy, secret word), nor near copies of them. 6 more relation types are held out to watch childhood.
  - **The tests' wordings are never used in childhood.**
  - So the tests ask about kinds of facts, and in words, that v5 never trained on.
  - 600 steps on this CPU, about 50 minutes.

**Smoke checks (not results):** after 30 childhood steps (layer 8), on a 10-person dev school: plain 92%, unseen
wordings 82%, two-step thinking out loud 95%, two-step asked directly 45%.

## The tests

1. **The school** (the other session's `brain/school.py`, unchanged world and wordings): 40 people × 5 facts, each
   taught once inside a story, with a story between lessons. Then the exam, one quiz per fact with a correction
   for each wrong answer, and the exam again. Exam questions (1 of 5 names, chance 20%):
   - plain wording
   - other words: the school's exam wording, never used in a lesson or in childhood
   - two-step, asked directly
   - **two-step, thinking out loud:** first "{s}'s best friend is" (1 of 5), then the chosen friend's fact (1 of 5).
     This is a fixed two-step plan, and RAG gets the same plan.
2. **The life test** (`brain/lifetest.py`, unchanged): the test stream, about 636 sessions, with the held-out
   wordings of `data.HELDOUT_PARAPHRASE`.

Baselines on the same frozen LLM: RAG, no learning and lookup (school and life). Fine-tuning is in the life test
only: its numbers are the hybrid run's (same LLM, same stream, same code).

## The dev choice (fixed now)

- Layers 8, 4 and 16 each get a 600-step childhood. Each is kept at its lowest dev-relation loss.
- **The layer:** the highest dev school score (after the quiz), plain + other words. A tie goes to the lower
  layer. The school uses dev names, world seed 0.
- Then one dev life with the chosen layer. It is reported only and changes nothing.
- The test stream and the test-name school run once, with the chosen layer.

## Pass rules (test names and the test stream)

| Rule | Passes when |
|---|---|
| V1 school, plain | ≥ 90% (after the quiz) |
| **V2 school, other words** | **≥ 60%** (after the quiz) |
| V3 school, two-step thinking out loud | ≥ 60% |
| V4 life: learns and keeps | recall after 3 readings ≥ 80%, early facts kept ≥ 0.80, bits per byte rise ≤ 5% |
| **V5 life, held-out wordings** | **≥ 60%** |

Overall pass: all five. Reported, not rules: everything against RAG, two-step asked directly, one reading,
corrections, changed facts, memory size, ms per session.

## Predictions (written before the runs)

- V1 passes (about 95%). V3 passes (about 80%).
- V2 passes, probably at 60–80%. 40 people make more interference than the smoke's 10.
- V4 passes. The gate should keep the bits per byte rise near 0.
- **V5 is the most uncertain.** The life test's held-out wordings ("{s} has a {pet} whose name is", "The friend
  {s} likes best is") are further from the lesson wordings than the school's. I predict 45–65%: a coin flip on
  passing.
- **RAG still wins** on plain and other words (about 100%) and ties on two-step thinking out loud. Two-step asked
  directly is low for both: neither chains facts without the plan.

## What happens next (fixed now)

- **V2 or V5 fails:** the "memory that keeps meaning" idea, as built, doesn't generalise far enough. Write up
  v1–v5. The memory becomes a product feature (learning each correction on the next request).
- **All pass:** for the first time Shishya's memory answers questions in words it never saw, about kinds of facts
  it never trained on, without gradients after childhood. Next: a larger LLM on a GPU, and tests where keeping
  text and looking it up struggles: many facts in one sentence, facts that change many times, and facts that must
  be combined without a fixed plan.
